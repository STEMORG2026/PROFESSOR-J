**State the problem in one sentence.**

Learners, researchers, and everyday users lack a single autonomous AI platform that
teaches with grounded, verified knowledge and Socratic scaffolding, supports research and
code sandboxing, and remains a general assistant — because existing AI tools either
hallucinate STEM facts, dump raw answers, or are single-purpose.

**Who experiences it, and under what conditions?**

STEM students (SEE/NEB/A-levels/undergrad) preparing for exams; independent researchers
verifying derivations and papers; educators generating curriculum-aligned content; and
general users wanting a unified personal AI assistant. All currently juggle generic chats
that lack grounding and verification.

**What evidence shows this is a real problem?**

Generic LLMs produce plausible-but-wrong formulas; they answer homework rather than
teaching; they cannot cite page-exact sources; they cannot safely run code to verify a
claim. JARVIS proved the platform mechanics but was not education-aware and had a name
with copyright exposure.

**What happens if it is not addressed?**

Learners keep being misled by hallucinated content, researchers lose trust in AI-assisted
verification, and the JARVIS capability base stagnates under a problematic name without a
generalized successor.

**What are the suspected root causes (not yet solutions)?**

Ungrounded generation by default; no pedagogical intent (answer-over-teach); no verified
citation layer; no safe execution sandbox; and siloed single-purpose tools.

**Where does the problem stop — what is explicitly outside its boundary?**

Guaranteeing factual accuracy for arbitrary non-grounded topics; replacing human teachers;
multi-tenant SaaS operations; payments.
